    About

    Finding out who owns your building should be simple. It usually isn't.

    The problem

    Many rental buildings in Vancouver aren't owned by a person you could name. They're owned by companies with names like "1234567 B.C. Ltd.", and those companies are often owned by other companies.

    1. Your building
    2. 1234567 B.C. Ltd.
    3. Another company
    4. ?

    The address on file might be a lawyer's office. Some of this is done on purpose, to make owners hard to trace.

    Why it stays hard

    The City's records are built to track property: what it sold for, what it's worth, what taxes are owed. They can't answer the questions tenants actually ask. Who is my landlord? What else do they own? Is my building about to be sold or torn down?

    So when a tenant does the digging and finds an answer, it usually stays with them. The next person in the same building has to start over.

    What we're building

    Tenant Commons is a shared place to keep those answers. It brings public data about each building onto one map, and tenants and organizers add what the records can't show.

    Where it stands

    This is a work in progress. The map already covers Vancouver's rental buildings, but the owner is still unknown for many of them, and a lot is still being built. We're sharing it early because the people who use it should help decide what it becomes.

    What the map can do

    • Find your building

      Units, year built, assessed land and building value, and issues on the City's property record.

    • Follow the owner

      Pick a landlord and see every building in their network, even when each one is held by its own numbered company.

    • Filter the city

      Narrow the map by units, year built, assessed value, or how many buildings a landlord holds.

    • Follow the evidence

      Every fact shows the record behind it: a licence, an assessment, an FOI release, or a tenant's report.

    • See the gaps

      Buildings whose ownership hasn't been found yet are marked, so you can see where the records run out.

    • Take it with you

      Browse buildings and landlords as tables and export them as CSV.
